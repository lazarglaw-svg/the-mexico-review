# Restaurant Guides — Editorial Voice & Integrity
Updated: 2026-10-15

## Tone
- Write natural, elegant, place-led English, with concise evocative openings, clear subheadings and practical visitor advice. The reader should feel guided by a skilled editor, not a database or compliance document.
- Avoid repetitive constructions such as "according to publicly available descriptions," "the published menu lists," "documentary profile," "research-based article" and conspicuous blanket disclaimer boxes.
- Contextualize the menu through geography, ingredients, history and culinary approach. Link restaurants' official booking and menu pages where helpful; keep links relevant and uncluttered.
- Never pose as an on-site reviewer without documented first-hand notes. Do not invent tasting notes, hospitality observations, prices, conversations, images, awards or personal experiences. Do not state or imply "we visited", "we tasted", "our server", "we found", "from our table" unless true and verified.
- Do not present a changing menu as guaranteed current; a discreet, natural "menus may change" note is enough where applicable.
- For new restaurant guides, **use authentic restaurant/interior/food photography only when licensed for our editorial website, or publish without a restaurant-specific image**. Do not generate AI food or venue images. Existing AI placeholders are legacy assets to replace after rights verification; until replacement, keep their illustration captions transparent. Do not present generic photos as images of a restaurant.
- Save photographer, file source, license/permission evidence, allowed uses, credit text and any changes alongside each approved asset. Public credit must comply with the photographer's license; ideally keep it discreet beneath the image.
- Keep article pages as `Article` in JSON-LD, not `Review` or `AggregateRating`, until there is evidence for an actual independent review.
- Include verifiable recognitions precisely: one Michelin star is different from a Michelin Guide selection.
- Keep a private editorial fact-check worksheet of source links and dates. Source citations need not dominate the public storytelling; factual assertions must remain defensible.
- Maintain metadata, canonical, internal links, image captions, structured data and SEO tests with every edit.
- Spanish and English are first-class languages of the platform; translate full articles when the editorial translation is ready, and do not invent alternate URLs prematurely.
