Mexico’s Best Bars in 2026: Four in the World’s Top 50

From a Juárez speakeasy to Guadalajara’s agave specialists, four extraordinary bars are reshaping Mexico’s place in the global cocktail conversation.

By The Mexico Review·October 20, 2026

The Mexican cocktail scene takes centre stage

Behind a discreet entrance in Mexico City’s Juárez neighbourhood, cocktails are developed with techniques associated with fine-dining kitchens. In Roma Norte, Italian aperitivo culture finds a new home; in Guadalajara, tequila and small producers are at the heart of the conversation. Mexico’s best-known bars increasingly stand out through distinct identities rather than a shared formula.

On October 7, 2026, the international The 50 Best Bars 2026 list was revealed in Milan. Four Mexican establishments placed in the global top 50: three in Mexico City and one in Guadalajara. These are distinctions from the 50 Best organisation, not awards granted by The Mexico Review.

The 2026 edition places two Mexican bars in the world’s top ten, an exceptional moment for the country’s contemporary cocktail culture. The published order is an industry ranking rather than our own rating of hospitality, flavours or service.

Handshake Speakeasy · No. 6

Where: Juárez, Mexico City. Known for: technical precision, layered cocktails and an intimate speakeasy setting.

Handshake Speakeasy previously reached the top of the world ranking in 2024. In 2026, it retains sixth place globally and receives the organisation’s Best Bar in North America award.

Its aesthetic recalls Art Deco-era private bars: low light, a theatrical entrance and a room with a sense of anticipation. Yet the defining idea is technique. Bartenders work with clarification, careful infusion and temperature control, transforming recognisable flavours into deceptively simple drinks. Reserving ahead is sensible; verify current arrangements on the venue’s official channels.

Bar Mauro · No. 10 and Highest New Entry

Where: Roma Norte, Mexico City. Known for: Italian aperitivo rituals, elegant design and a social evening.

Bar Mauro entered the top ten in 2026 and also earned the Highest New Entry distinction. Vermouth, amaro and Negroni variations sit comfortably alongside Mexican ingredients and a warm, design-conscious space.

The bar is an invitation to linger. While some destinations impress through elaborate equipment, Mauro centres its appeal on hospitality, conversation and a clear point of view about how an evening should unfold. It is a useful reminder that a great bar can feel welcoming rather than theatrical.

El Gallo Altanero · No. 28

Where: Guadalajara, Jalisco. Known for: tequila heritage and independent agave-spirit producers.

The only Guadalajara address among Mexico’s 2026 top 50, El Gallo Altanero makes a case for regional expertise. Here the story begins with tequila, neighbouring agave traditions and the people who produce them.

Instead of relying exclusively on universally recognisable labels, the bar encourages discovery through small producers and the distinctions between Mexican spirits. It offers a particularly meaningful way to encounter Jalisco beyond its most familiar clichés. Travellers combining dining and drinks in Guadalajara can also explore our Allium restaurant guide.

Tlecān · No. 47

Where: Roma Norte, Mexico City. Known for: mezcal, Mexican spirits and a lively, intimate mood.

Tlecān places Mexican identity at the centre of its drinks programme, pairing agave-based spirits with contemporary technique. Its name evokes fire in Náhuatl, while the bar’s visual language draws on Mexican cultural references.

Mezcal, other regional distillates and reinterpretations of drinks such as the Paloma set the tone. Rather than a vast luxury lounge, expect a smaller, energetic setting where ingredient origins and distinct flavours remain in focus.

Beyond the 50 and a considered itinerary

The expanded 51–100 selection includes further Mexican names such as Form + Matter and Licorería Limantour. Together, the rankings reflect a cocktail culture that has been evolving for many years rather than a one-season phenomenon.

For Mexico City, consider a Roma Norte evening around Bar Mauro or Tlecān, with Handshake in Juárez reserved for a separate night. Trying to visit all three hurriedly misses much of the pleasure. Check opening hours and reservation rules before setting out. If your trip extends to Guadalajara, El Gallo Altanero deserves time on its own terms.

Those planning a longer stay around the upcoming Formula 1 and Día de Muertos weekend will find the city’s bars a compelling part of an evening itinerary.

What Mexico’s best bars tell us about the country

Regional ingredients are now central characters, not token decorations. Bar design, music and service are increasingly distinctive. Bartending borrows confidently from contemporary cooking. Most importantly, being unmistakably Mexican is an international strength.

Handshake, Bar Mauro, El Gallo Altanero and Tlecān are not versions of the same luxury bar. Their differences are the reason Mexico’s cocktail scene deserves attention.

Frequently asked questions

Which Mexican bars ranked in the world’s top 50 in 2026?

Handshake Speakeasy (No. 6), Bar Mauro (No. 10), El Gallo Altanero (No. 28) and Tlecān (No. 47).

Which is the highest-ranked bar in Mexico?

Handshake Speakeasy, ranked sixth worldwide and named Best Bar in North America by the ranking organisation.

What are the highest-ranked bars in Mexico City?

Handshake Speakeasy, Bar Mauro and Tlecān, at Nos. 6, 10 and 47 respectively.

Which Guadalajara bar appears on the list?

El Gallo Altanero, ranked No. 28 in 2026.

Policies vary by venue and date. Handshake in particular is best approached as a planned booking.
