Esquina Común: Creative Mexican Dining in Condesa

An intimate leafy rooftop, chef Ana Dolores González and a menu that changes with the kitchen’s ideas.

By The Mexico Review editorial desk · Research updated October 15, 2026 · Research-based profile

Editorial transparency: This independently researched profile is not a firsthand restaurant visit, an awarded TMR selection or a paid placement. Dishes are documented from linked sources and may change. The hero image is an AI-generated illustration, not a photograph from the venue.

The concept

One of the most distinctive traits of Esquina Común is its scale. Rather than a grand dining room, the restaurant occupies a covered, greenery-filled rooftop in Condesa. Michelin describes a relaxed setting and a tasting-menu-like approach, with dishes portioned for two. Chef Ana Dolores González leads the kitchen.

The restaurant began in 2021 in a small space in Roma, according to the owners' own description on OpenTable, before moving to the Condesa rooftop. Its approach emphasizes the ingredient, technical skill and changing combinations rather than a permanent list of house classics.

Recognition: Esquina Común retained one Michelin star in the 2026 Michelin Guide Mexico. This is verified against Michelin's official 2026 list, not inferred from review-site badges.

What to look for on the menu

Important: These are documented past dishes, not a verified live menu. Esquina Común evolves its offerings, so you should ask for the current selection before booking for a particular dish.

1. Fish of the day with mole verde — described by Michelin

Michelin highlights a preparation in which grilled cod may be served with a rich green mole, ripe plantain, fava beans and pumpkin seeds. Why it matters: it illustrates a contemporary interpretation of ingredients and sauces familiar in Mexican cooking.

2. Fish tamal with salsa macha — described by Michelin

The guide also calls out a fish tamal combining masa and plantain, filled with chile-seasoned fish and accompanied by salsa macha. Why it matters: a recognizable Mexican format handled through a creative, technique-led lens.

3. Tuna tostada — documented in May 2026

Food & Wine en Español published a May 2026 restaurant visit describing a corn tostada with fresh tuna, potato and chile güero. Why it matters: a useful example of the kitchen's contrast between crisp masa, seafood and fresh components. This is not confirmed as a current offering.

4. Pork barbacoa sope — documented in May 2026

The same critic describes a pork barbacoa sope with pasilla chile salsa, beans and avocado. Why it matters: the restaurant's creativity does not exclude classic Mexican preparations.

5. Seafood crudo — documented by the critic

The article also mentions a seafood crudo with shrimp, passion fruit and lemongrass, with scallop served during that visit. Why it matters: the kitchen is not limited to traditional combinations; it works with acidity and different culinary influences.

What the experience is like

Michelin's description emphasizes the terrace atmosphere and individuality of the cooking, while OpenTable guests widely praise the inventive approach and service. A tasting-style meal and changing dishes may not be ideal for someone seeking predictable comfort food, very large portions or a fixed signature item.

This is a small, popular restaurant. Booking well ahead is sensible. Michelin's venue profile mentions Instagram direct messages for reservations, while OpenTable also hosts a live restaurant listing; guests should verify the currently accepted booking method.

Who should consider it?

Esquina Común is an excellent research shortlist choice for people interested in contemporary Mexican cooking, inventive uses of masa and seafood, and a relaxed approach to Michelin-level dining. Its star is an independently verifiable accolade; our restaurant profile is not a claim of a firsthand TMR tasting.
