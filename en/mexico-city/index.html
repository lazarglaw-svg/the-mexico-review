Getting to know the destination

Many cities within one.

Mexico City is the nation’s capital, set on the central plateau at approximately 2,240 metres above sea level. As a federal entity it covers about 1,494 km² and is divided into 16 administrative boroughs. Its scale means that visiting different districts can feel like exploring several cities at once.

The Historic Centre brings together major public squares, museums and landmarks. Roma and Condesa are known for cafés, restaurants, leafy parks and early twentieth-century architecture. Coyoacán has a more traditional atmosphere of plazas, neighbourhood streets and cultural spaces. To the west, Polanco combines museums, commerce and dining.

Areas & neighbourhoods

Neighbourhoods to know.

Each part of the capital has its own rhythm. These three offer a useful starting point.

01

Historic Centre

The monumental core, home to historic architecture, museums, plazas and some of Mexico’s most recognisable streets.

02

Roma & Condesa

Tree-lined avenues, eclectic architecture, cafés and restaurants. Parque México and Parque España are local landmarks.

03

Coyoacán

Squares, markets, narrow streets and cultural traditions give this area a distinct feel away from the capital’s high-rise districts.
